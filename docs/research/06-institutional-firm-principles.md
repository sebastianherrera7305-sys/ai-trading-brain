# 06 — Institutional Firm Principles: What We Adopt from the World's Best

Survey of publicly documented engineering/research/risk principles from the
leading quantitative firms, and what AI Trading Brain should adopt. Sources:
public interviews, papers, disclosures, conferences — nothing proprietary.
Documentation only; no implementation in this document.

---

## 1. Renaissance Technologies (Medallion)
- **Data paranoia:** every data point is suspect until provenance-verified;
  restatements are modeled, not ignored.
- **Research as assembly line:** signals generated by hundreds of
  independent researchers, each with tiny, thoroughly validated slices;
  combination is where alpha lives.
- **Statistical discipline:** everything is an experiment with a controlled
  comparison; nothing ships on intuition.
- **Secrecy of process:** infrastructure is deliberately boring, predictable,
  auditable — the edge is in the data pipeline hygiene, not clever code.
- **Adopt:** content-hashed snapshot discipline (already in ADD §40);
  independent signal ownership with portfolio-level combination (not a
  single monolithic strategy); boring infra.

## 2. Two Sigma
- **Breadth over depth:** thousands of modest, diverse strategies beat a
  handful of bold bets; risk is diversified at the research level.
- **Data-driven decisioning everywhere:** even operational decisions get
  data treatment; investment decisions are tested like hypotheses.
- **Engineering as first-class citizen:** the platform is the moat; product
  and research teams share the same rigor.
- **Adopt:** research breadth via the Registry's many-claims model + BH-FDR
  hygiene; treat platform quality as risk management (docs 04/05).

## 3. Citadel Securities
- **Market-making edge is speed and inventory discipline:** quoting math,
  inventory skew, adverse-selection controls — not directional views.
- **Extreme engineering:** latency budgets treated as engineering
  specifications; every microsecond measured.
- **Risk as a function, not a rule book:** risk is real-time, algorithmic,
  and autonomous (auto-hedges, auto-reductions).
- **Adopt:** real-time algorithmic risk controls (Subsystem 3 validators);
  measure execution quality with the same rigor as strategy performance.

## 4. Jane Street
- **OCaml culture / correctness obsession:** the whole stack is built so
  that "wrong" is nearly impossible; types, invariants, and reviews.
- **Collaborative, fast iteration:** small teams, code review culture,
  postmortems without blame.
- **Market-making + stat-arb blend:** edge from microstructure and relative
  value, with massive data throughput.
- **Adopt:** correctness-first engineering (strong typing, invariants,
  append-only audit trails — already in our design); postmortem culture on
  every unexpected loss; relative-value/stat-arb research lines (doc 01).

## 5. AQR (quantamental institutional)
- **Theory-first research:** every factor/strategy starts from economic
  rationale; empirical findings without theory are suspect.
- **Discipline and transparency for LPs:** documented, repeatable process;
  client-grade reporting of risks and attribution.
- **Factor diversification:** value, momentum, carry, defensive — held
  together by risk parity-style weighting.
- **Adopt:** theory-first gate for new strategies (research metadata:
  hypothesis_id — the findings table requires the stated mechanism);
  institutional-grade reporting/attribution as the Reporting layer's
  standard (Part III §38).

## 6. D.E. Shaw
- **"We don't have secrets, we have process":** the moat is the systematic,
  repeatable process that produces ideas — not any single idea.
- **Skepticism and falsification:** teams actively try to kill their own
  strategies; surviving falsification is the promotion criterion.
- **Adopt:** adversarial review of our own findings (the SPRT kill-switch
  in doc 05 is the operational form); promotion requires surviving attempts
  to falsify.

## 7. Jump Trading / HRT / Tower / IMC / Optiver (HFT/market making)
- **Latency is a feature:** co-location, FPGA, kernel bypass; every layer
  measured in microseconds.
- **Inventory and queue management:** small per-trade edge, huge volume,
  strict inventory caps.
- **Engineering culture of ownership:** small teams own entire subsystems
  end-to-end; deployment discipline is uniform (no "research code" vs
  "production code" — one pipeline).
- **Adopt:** uniform deployment pipeline (no research/production split —
  already a design principle in our ADD); strict position caps (ADR-0003
  portfolio-level limits); ownership culture per subsystem.

## 8. XTX Markets
- **Systematic, no discretionary overlay:** decisions entirely algorithmic;
  humans set parameters, not trade.
- **Efficiency obsession:** cost per trade and per unit of risk minimized
  at every layer (they publish the lowest implicit costs in FX).
- **Adopt:** full automation of decisions (AI Decision Engine emits
  decisions, humans govern thresholds — per ADD §25); continuous
  cost-of-execution measurement (doc 04 execution_features).

## 9. AQR/RenTech/Tower commonalities (the synthesis)
1. **Process > ideas** — repeatable, documented, falsifiable pipeline.
2. **Data hygiene is risk management** — provenance, restatement handling,
   immutable snapshots.
3. **Many small bets** — diversified edge portfolio, not concentrated bets.
4. **Algorithmic risk controls** — automated, not discretionary.
5. **No research/production split** — one disciplined pipeline.
6. **Postmortems and pre-mortems** — institutional memory of failure.
7. **Statistical honesty** — every claim carries its test battery (doc 05).

## 11. What we deliberately do NOT adopt

- RenTech-style secrecy (we are public-by-design; our moat is process + data,
  not concealment).
- HFT micro-latency hardware (co-location, FPGA) — incompatible with our
  institutional-investor market segment; we adopt their engineering
  *discipline*, not their latency arms race.
- DE Shaw's internal-investor-only funding model — we are a platform
  product, not a fund; the "process moat" lesson applies, the business model
  does not.

## 12. Bottom line

The institutions converge on seven principles, and every one maps cleanly
onto a component AI Trading Brain already has in its ADD (Registry, findings
table, Edge Monitor, immutable snapshots, algorithmic validators). This
document confirms the architecture's direction: no new components required —
the missing piece is discipline of application, which docs 01-05 specify in
operational form.
